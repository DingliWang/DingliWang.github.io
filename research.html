Conditional Distributionally Robust Estimation with Wasserstein Ambiguity

Dingli Wang Under review, Journal of Econometrics

How can we estimate models that remain reliable when the conditional distribution of outcomes shifts away from the one observed in the data?

▶ Abstract

I develop distributionally robust M-estimation under a conditional Wasserstein ambiguity set that fixes the marginal distribution of covariates $P_X$ while allowing the conditional law of $Y \mid X$ to be perturbed. A pilot-anchored regularization scheme makes the resulting estimator feasible and stable in finite samples, and I establish its large-sample properties. The theory is applied to U.S. Growth-at-Risk.

Robust Replacement of Human Decision Makers under Covariate Shift

Dingli Wang Under review, Journal of Econometrics

▶ Abstract

When should a machine-learning algorithm replace a human decision maker once the deployment environment differs from the one in which it was trained? Modeling the distribution shift through a conditional Wasserstein ambiguity set, I characterize a distributionally robust ROC frontier and derive a finite-sample welfare-regret bound that yields a feasible rule for when automation dominates human judgment.

Sharp Targeting with Generated States: Boundary Traces and the Validation Frontier

Dingli Wang Working paper

▶ Abstract

Many policy rules rank units using generated scores. For sharp targeting, ordinary prediction accuracy is the wrong validation criterion: errors far from the cutoff carry little first-order welfare relevance, so the object that matters is boundary-local. I show that fixed cutoffs depend on a welfare-weighted boundary trace, fixed-capacity rules on a boundary covariance, and learned cutoffs on a residualized trace-slope. For learned cutoffs, evaluation-sample cube-root uncertainty and root-$m$ validation uncertainty meet at the frontier $m \asymp n^{2/3}$: validation error is first order there and negligible only when $m \gg n^{2/3}$. For a finite-dimensional calibration layer over a fixed representation, I provide primitive influence functions and feasible validation-adjusted inference.
