PhD Candidate in Economics, University of California, Riverside

On the 2027–2028 economics job market

I am a PhD candidate in Economics at the University of California, Riverside. My research is in econometric theory, with applications to time series, forecasting, and machine learning in economics.

I develop estimators and inference procedures that stay reliable when the data-generating environment shifts or is only partially known — building robustness directly into estimation and delivering valid inference for time series, forecasting, and distributional questions. My job market paper develops distributionally robust estimation under a conditional Wasserstein ambiguity set, so that models stay reliable when the conditional distribution of outcomes departs from the data.
