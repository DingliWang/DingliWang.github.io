Robust Replacement of Human Decision Makers under Covariate Shift

Dingli Wang Revise and Resubmit, Journal of Econometrics

When should a machine-learning algorithm replace a human decision maker once the deployment environment differs from the environment in which it was trained?

▶ Abstract

I study when a machine-learning algorithm should replace a human decision maker once the deployment environment differs from the environment in which it was trained. Modeling this distribution shift through a conditional Wasserstein ambiguity set, I characterize a distributionally robust ROC frontier and derive a finite-sample welfare-regret bound that governs the replacement decision and yields a feasible rule for when automation dominates human judgment.

When Should Forecasts Be Equally Weighted? A Signal–Cost Diagnostic for Forecast Combination

Dingli Wang Working Paper

▶ Abstract

Forecast combinations with estimated weights can fail to improve on equal weights when estimation error offsets the population gain from unequal weighting. I develop a signal–cost diagnostic that separates the gain from unequal weighting from the finite-sample cost of estimating the combination, with the goal of identifying when the data support moving away from equal weights.

Median-Anchored Adjustment of Joint VaR–ES Forecasts

Dingli Wang and Tae-Hwy Lee Under Review, Journal of Business & Economic Statistics

▶ Abstract

We study a low-dimensional post-estimation adjustment for joint Value-at-Risk and Expected Shortfall forecasts. The adjustment is anchored at the conditional median and is designed to improve joint tail-risk forecasts without re-estimating the underlying forecasting model.

Adversarial Density Forecast for Macro-financial Risks

Dingli Wang and Tae-Hwy Lee Working Paper

▶ Abstract

We develop an adversarial density-forecasting approach for macro-financial risk in small samples. The method anchors training to economically relevant distributional features so that the resulting density forecasts remain stable while retaining the flexibility of adversarial learning.
