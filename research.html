Robust Replacement of Human Decision Makers under Distribution Shift

Revise and Resubmit, Journal of Econometrics SSRN PDF

I study when a machine should replace a human before its deployment distribution is known. A robust ROC frontier evaluates fixed thresholds over a label-preserving Wasserstein set and separates the replacement decision from threshold choice.

Abstract

We study when a machine should replace a human before its deployment distribution is known. A robust receiver operating characteristic frontier evaluates fixed thresholds over a label-preserving Wasserstein set, while historical decisions give a posterior for human performance, assumed stable across environments. Replacement requires a specified posterior probability of no higher false-positive rate and no lower true-positive rate throughout the set. We establish uniform frontier estimation and welfare bounds that separate whom to replace from which threshold to deploy. Threshold choice can leave a persistent welfare difference from a deployment benchmark that matches the human’s false-positive rate, even with a known frontier and increasingly informative histories. For logistic scores, average and per-observation displacement budgets affect threshold probabilities at square-root and linear local orders, respectively. Simulations holding the score fixed show partial replacement and positive mean gains at evaluated distributions within the per-observation set, while the corresponding average budgets yield retention. Choosing thresholds by a welfare lower bound within the same posterior feasible set improves mean deployment welfare in this design.

Median-Anchored Adjustment of Joint VaR–ES Forecasts with Tae-Hwy Lee

Under Review, Journal of Business & Economic StatisticsSSRNPDF

A one-parameter adjustment to existing tail-risk forecasts that rescales their distances from the fitted median, preserves VaR–ES ordering, and has a closed-form spread-weighted quantile estimator.

Abstract

Risk managers often work with a fitted forecasting model they cannot replace even when its tail forecasts need adjustment. We propose a median-anchored rule that multiplies the distances from the fitted median to Value-at-Risk (VaR) and Expected Shortfall (ES) by a common positive multiplier. The rule preserves VaR–ES ordering, and minimizing VaR check loss gives a closed-form weighted-quantile estimator. We establish consistency, give an asymptotic distribution under high-level conditions accounting for baseline estimation, and derive a VaR coverage-error bound at the forecast origin. When the same multiplier correctly adjusts both VaR and ES, the adjusted pair has lower conditional zero-homogeneous Fissler–Ziegel (FZ0) risk at that origin. Monte Carlo experiments examine this condition and several forms of misspecification. In rolling S&P 500 forecasts the adjustment lowers FZ0 loss in all four GARCH cells, each pairwise significant, with the largest and most robust gain, 10.4%, for Gaussian GARCH at the 1% tail; the 5% gains do not survive the most conservative family-wise adjustments. A quantile-regression baseline marks the boundary: when the fitted tail is already flexible, one multiplier adds little. Filtered historical simulation quantifies what standardized residuals and conditional scales add when available.

Adversarial Density Forecast for Macro-financial Risks with Tae-Hwy Lee

An anchored conditional Wasserstein generative model for density forecasting in short macroeconomic samples, with theory and evidence on distributional accuracy and sensitivity to training randomness.

Abstract

Short estimation windows can make adversarial density forecasts sensitive to network size and training randomness. We study a conditional Wasserstein generative adversarial network with a penalty that matches the variance and five quantiles of simulated and observed outcomes within training blocks. We analyze the expected penalty with finitely many simulation draws. Its variance and median components bound average generated second moments and, under weak dependence and approximation conditions, yield prediction risk bounds that also control excess expected continuous ranked probability score (CRPS). After reducing network size, the penalty lowers Wasserstein error in all four simulation settings and average variation across fits by about 25 percent. A relativistic GAN provides competitive accuracy but greater variation across fits. In three macroeconomic applications, the penalty improves CRPS point estimates in five of six same-capacity comparisons; classical benchmarks remain strong and short-window tail coverage is poor.

When Should Forecasts Be Equally Weighted? Signal Recovery and Estimation Costs

A comparison of equal and estimated forecast weights that separates recovered predictive signal from estimation costs, accounts for tuning and component estimation, and uses past out-of-sample losses to guide the choice.

Abstract

Whether estimated combination weights improve on equal weights depends not only on the signal in forecast differences, but also on how much of that signal the forecasting procedure recovers and at what cost. We express the expected mean squared forecast error (MSFE) gain in terms of signal recovery and estimation costs, giving a boundary for when a specified forecasting procedure improves on equal weighting. We complement this theoretical boundary with a decision rule based on past out-of-sample loss differences to choose between equal weights and the estimated weights. Under stationarity and weak dependence, with estimation windows fixed, the probability of choosing the lower-MSFE method tends to one as past evaluations accumulate, provided the two population MSFEs differ. Simulations and macroeconomic applications illustrate the comparison and the choices of equal and estimated weights.
