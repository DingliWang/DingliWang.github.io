Robust Replacement of Human Decision Makers under Covariate Shift

Dingli Wang Revise and Resubmit, Journal of Econometrics

I study when a machine-learning algorithm should replace a human decision maker when the deployment distribution differs from the training distribution. I construct a thresholdwise robust ROC frontier using a Wasserstein ambiguity set and derive a finite-sample welfare-regret bound for replacement decisions.

Abstract

I study when a machine-learning algorithm should replace a human decision maker once the deployment environment differs from the environment in which it was trained. The analysis builds a distributionally robust ROC frontier from worst-case true-positive and false-positive rates over a Wasserstein ambiguity set. For Lipschitz score functions, I derive a finite-sample welfare-regret bound that links frontier estimation error to replacement mistakes and welfare loss.

Median-Anchored Adjustment of Joint VaR–ES Forecasts with Tae-Hwy Lee

Under Review, Journal of Business & Economic Statistics

We study a median-anchored rule that multiplies the distances from the fitted median to Value-at-Risk and Expected Shortfall by a common positive multiplier. The rule preserves VaR–ES ordering and has a closed-form spread-weighted quantile estimator.

Abstract

Risk managers often work with a fitted forecasting model that cannot be replaced even when its tail forecasts need adjustment. We study a median-anchored adjustment that rescales the fitted median-to-VaR and median-to-ES distances by a common positive multiplier. The paper establishes large-sample properties, gives a next-origin coverage bound, and evaluates the method in rolling equity-index forecasts.

Adversarial Density Forecast for Macro-financial Risks with Tae-Hwy Lee

Working Paper

We study conditional adversarial density forecasts in short macroeconomic samples. The method adds distributional anchors based on the variance and selected quantiles to a conditional Wasserstein generative model, with the goal of reducing sensitivity to network size and training randomness.

Abstract

Short estimation windows can make adversarial density forecasts sensitive to network size and training randomness. We study an anchored conditional Wasserstein generative model that matches selected distributional features of simulated and observed outcomes. The paper develops prediction-risk bounds and evaluates the method in simulations and rolling forecasts of industrial production, unemployment, and inflation.

When Should Forecasts Be Equally Weighted? Signal Recovery and Estimation Costs with Xinran Liu

Working Paper

We study when estimated forecast-combination weights improve on equal weighting. The analysis separates recovered predictive signal from estimation costs and examines how tuning and component estimation affect the comparison.

Abstract

Whether estimated combination weights improve on equal weights depends not only on the signal in forecast differences, but also on how much of that signal the forecasting procedure recovers and at what cost. We express the expected mean squared forecast error gain in terms of signal recovery and estimation costs and use past out-of-sample loss differences to guide the choice between equal weights and an estimated-weight combination.
